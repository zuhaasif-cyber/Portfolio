I’m an interaction designer with 3 years of experience, and I specialize in making complex enterprise systems feel intuitive and human.

My work has lived in the unglamorous but deeply important corners of enterprise software — inventory management, cost estimation, quality control. And what keeps me going is knowing that when I get it right, I’m making the daily work of thousands of people in warehouses and operations teams a little less frustrating, a little more dignified.

These days, I’m especially drawn to the intersection of AI and enterprise UX — designing intelligent systems that don’t just automate tasks, but genuinely support the people using them. I believe AI is only as good as the experience wrapped around it, and that’s where I want to be.

Outside of work, I paint and illustrate — mostly botanicals, things I find in nature. It’s where I slow down, pay attention to detail, and let observation become something visual. Honestly, it’s not that different from design.

My last 3 years was in a large warehouse; not literally but close enough

Inventory

Where goods begin their journey

I designed workflows for how items move — from supplier to warehouse to customer. Receiving, put-away, storage, pick, pack, ship. The kind of flows that run silently in the background of every product you’ve ever ordered.

Quality

Where standards get enforced

Inspection plans, quality characteristics, result collection for maintenance work orders — I designed for the people whose job is to catch what others miss. Precision matters here, and so does clarity.

Costing

Where numbers meet reality

Cost estimation is complex — purchased items, manufactured goods, overhead rates, cost rollups. I worked on making these processes navigable for users who live inside spreadsheets and ERP screens all day.

I think in systems, and I paint what I notice.

Let’s talk — about systems, flowers, or both.
